# Quality gate | Pre-publish rubric

Score each criterion **0–5**. These scoring weights are a proposed working rule, not a founder-approved mathematical formula.

| Criterion | Weight | What 5/5 means |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Substantive truth / utility | 30 | A defensible, non-obvious insight useful for a real decision |
| Single-sentence phrasing | 20 | Directive, beautifully economical, immediately clear, memorable |
| Visual-semantic match | 20 | A precise picture that makes the line hit harder |
| Brand visual consistency | 15 | Single pale-sage/dark-green hand-ink composition, elegant legible italic |
| Distinctiveness / originality | 10 | Not generic AI style, not derivative or recycled proverb |
| Caption usefulness | 5 | Direct "why it matters" implication, not fluff |
| **Total** | **100** | Aim >=85 and no hard-gate failures |

Score calculation: weighted sum of each 0-5 score divided by 5. `>=85` means candidate for approval, not automatic publication. The user may reject a high-scoring work.

## Hard-fail conditions
- No actual useful insight or advice.
- Two separate sentences on image, or words differ from exact approved copy.
- Unintelligible metaphor, especially when it accidentally argues the opposite.
- Multi-panel collage or contact sheet submitted as a finished post.
- Colors outside 2–3 greens without permission.
- Illegible cursive or unreadably small text.
- Unoriginal imitation of another creator's distinctive artwork.
- Art approved/published claim without explicit recorded signoff.
- Fabricated source/attribution, or demonstrably false or harmful universal advice.
- Caption adds no practical substance.

## Review questions
1. What does the advice **actually tell the reader to do or avoid**?
2. Would a skeptical intelligent reader find it more insightful than a generic quote?
3. Is the one-sentence construction grammatical and under control?
4. Can a viewer explain the visual before reading the caption?
5. Does the image reveal tension or consequence, not just objects named in the line?
6. Does this add range to the last ten posts?
7. Would we still be proud to print this in a book?
