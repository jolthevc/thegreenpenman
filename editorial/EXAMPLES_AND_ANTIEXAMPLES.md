## Special warning about visual-semantic alignment

Bad: "Do not water what only learned how to take from you" next to a **wilted plant**. Plants depend on water; the picture accidentally makes the advice look like neglect.
Better: a hose drawing water *away* from the giver, or a person watering a vessel that drains back into someone else's jug. Show exploitative one-way transfer clearly, or abandon the metaphor.

Bad: "Never mistake being spared for being safe" with a harmless-looking sleeping cat and smiling mouse. If the threatening power isn't visually legible, the moral doesn't work.
Better: an awake cat gently holding a mouse under one paw, clearly capable of changing its mind.

Bad: "The cage was open. The bird had forgotten the sky." Two sentences, mainly an observation.
Better: "Choose the sky before the cage begins to look like home." One sentence, advice.

Bad: image depicting a large mountain landscape with a tiny generic figure and metaphorically unrelated caption.
Better: one close, expressive focal event. Details exist only if they communicate the insight.

## Every idea should come with
- Underlying lesson in ordinary language
- Exactly one on-image sentence
- Illustration with subject, gesture, critical visual relationship, and nothing extraneous
- "Why it matters" caption, 1–2 sentences
- Status and ID
